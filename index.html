Why Kashmir, why now

Kashmir grows most of India's apples. India still runs short.

Long, cold valley winters give apple trees the chill they need to flower evenly, and cool nights before harvest build colour and crunch. The country eats more apples than it grows, imports make up the gap at a protected floor price, and almost none of the valley has moved to modern planting yet.

The orchard

Two ways to grow a Kashmir apple

Most of the valley still grows tall trees on seedling rootstock. A high-density orchard uses compact trees on dwarfing rootstock, trained on a trellis with drip irrigation. It fruits years earlier and fetches a higher price at the orchard gate.

The price question

Price drives the answer, so here is the evidence

The farmgate price matters more than any other number in the model. These are the sources we checked in August and September 2026, on both sides.

What could pull it down

  • A wave of new Gala planting.

    J&K targets 5,500 ha in five years at a 50% subsidy, and about 4,000 ha a year is already going in under high- and medium-density schemes.

    NITI Aayog roadmap / J&K Budget 2026–27
  • Today's premium is a scarcity premium.

    ₹100–150/kg exists because high-density is 1% of the area. New Gala blocks bear from Year 2.

    Meeraas analysis
  • Import floors are not always enforced.

    Iranian and Turkish apples landed at ₹41 and ₹58/kg in 2023–24 despite the ₹50 minimum.

    HP Samyukt Kisan Manch
  • Trade deals are opening up.

    A US quota is agreed, and an April–August duty-cut window is under discussion.

    Business Today, Feb 2026
  • Weather and roads.

    Repeated hailstorms across south Kashmir in July–August 2026, and NH-44 closures at peak harvest.

    Greater Kashmir

How we read it

We plan below today's price, and we spread the risk

Our base case of ₹80/kg sits well under the ₹100–150/kg farmgate range, and the cautious case of ₹70/kg is about 40% under it. The bigger risk is the Gala premium shrinking as high-density planting grows from 1% toward 10% of the valley's apple area.

That is why we recommend mixing varieties in every block, with Fuji, Red Velox, Jeromine and Scarlet Spur alongside Gala. To see the downside, pick the Stress scenario: at ₹50/kg with no price growth, the model still pays back in Year 7, though margins get thin late in the 20 years as costs keep rising.

Questions

What clients ask us first

What is a kanal?

A land unit used in Jammu & Kashmir. Our model uses 5,500 sq ft per kanal, so about 8 kanals make an acre. Each kanal holds 180 trees.

Do I need to buy land?

No. The model assumes leased land at ₹18,000 per kanal in Year 1, rising 10% every two years. If you already own land in the valley, open the cost assumptions in the calculator and set the lease to zero.

When does the orchard start paying?

The first light crop comes in Year 2 and the orchard reaches full bearing in Year 5. In the base case your cumulative cash position turns positive in Year 5.

Are these returns guaranteed?

No. They are projections. The result depends most on the price your fruit sells for, then on yield and weather. Use the calculator to test a ₹50/kg stress case, lower the yield, or add the cost of hail netting.

Who sells the fruit?

We do. Our 15% commission comes out of the sale proceeds, so we earn more only when your fruit sells for more.

What happens in a hail year?

Hailstorms hit south Kashmir repeatedly in July and August 2026. Anti-hail netting protects the crop but is not in the base model. Enter its cost per kanal in the calculator: the model charges it in Year 1 and again in the replacement year you choose, Year 12 by default.

Why Gala, and why mix varieties?

Gala on dwarfing rootstock fruits in its second year and currently earns a premium at the farmgate. Because many new orchards are planting Gala, we recommend mixing in Fuji, Red Velox, Jeromine and Scarlet Spur so your harvest does not depend on one variety's price.

How are IRR and NPV worked out?

Both use the year-by-year net cashflow shown in the calculator, with the Year-1 investment as the first year's outflow. IRR is the yearly return at which those cashflows balance to zero. NPV discounts each year at your hurdle rate, 12% by default. Figures are before tax and assume no sale value for the orchard at the end of the period.
